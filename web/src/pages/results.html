Results of the sealed test evaluation

Three methods were frozen on training/validation data (commit {{results.freeze_commit}}) and evaluated exactly once on two sealed test sets of {{results.splits.test_iid.n|int}} records each: test_iid (drawn like the training data) and test_shift (lower SNR, wider frequency range, more harmonics and unbalance, ±2 % calibration error and an unseen transient). Every number on this page comes from reports/results.json. All results are [SIM]. Stage 2 retrained the model on representative conditions and tested it on new sealed data: see Experiment 2 at the end of this page.

Headline

Macro-F1 averages the F1 score over the seven classes. ML methods: mean ± sd over three training seeds. False alarm = a normal record labelled as an event; miss = an event labelled normal.

Are the differences real?

Intervals: paired, class-stratified bootstrap 95 % confidence intervals of the macro-F1 difference (seed mean); McNemar: exact test on the records where exactly one of the two methods is right (per seed). "Better" is claimed only if the CI excludes zero.

Findings, in plain words

  1. On data like the training data, both are equally good. test_iid: GBM-embedded {{results.splits.test_iid.methods.gbm_embedded.macro_f1.mean|f4}} vs rules {{results.splits.test_iid.methods.rules.macro_f1.mean|f4}}; difference {{results.splits.test_iid.comparisons.gbm_embedded_minus_rules.diff|signed4}}, 95 % CI {{results.splits.test_iid.comparisons.gbm_embedded_minus_rules.ci95|ci4}} — no significant difference.
  2. Under shift the rules are significantly better. test_shift: rules {{results.splits.test_shift.methods.rules.macro_f1.mean|f4}}, GBM-embedded {{results.splits.test_shift.methods.gbm_embedded.macro_f1.mean|f4}} (difference {{results.splits.test_shift.comparisons.gbm_embedded_minus_rules.diff|signed4}}, CI {{results.splits.test_shift.comparisons.gbm_embedded_minus_rules.ci95|ci4}}). From iid to shift the rules lose {{results.iid_to_shift.rules.change|signed3}}, the tree model {{results.iid_to_shift.gbm_embedded.change|signed3}}. When noise and nuisances leave the training range, the learned model degrades sharply; the rules barely move.
  3. Where the model fails: phase jumps and false alarms at low SNR. GBM-embedded (seed 0) on test_shift recognised only {{results.derived.test_shift__gbm_embedded__jump_recall|pct0}} of phase jumps ({{results.derived.test_shift__gbm_embedded__jump_to_normal|int}} called normal, {{results.derived.test_shift__gbm_embedded__jump_to_osc|int}} oscillation) and flagged {{results.derived.test_shift__gbm_embedded__normal_flagged|int}} normal records ({{results.derived.test_shift__gbm_embedded__normal_to_osc|int}} as oscillation). Seed mean: {{results.splits.test_shift.methods.gbm_embedded.false_alarm_rate.mean|pct1}} false alarms, {{results.splits.test_shift.methods.gbm_embedded.miss_rate.mean|pct1}} misses. The model learned noise-dependent shortcuts; the physical jump threshold does not depend on noise.
  4. A bigger model did not help. GBM-unconstrained: test_iid {{results.splits.test_iid.methods.gbm_unconstrained.macro_f1.mean|f4}}, test_shift {{results.splits.test_shift.methods.gbm_unconstrained.macro_f1.mean|f4}} with {{results.splits.test_shift.methods.gbm_unconstrained.false_alarm_rate.mean|pct1}} false alarms.
  5. Safety-critical confusions stay at zero for the rules and GBM-embedded (phase jump vs frequency event, sag vs normal), while the naive PLL rule flags {{results.splits.test_shift.naive_pll.by_class.phase_jump.rate|pct0}} of phase jumps as frequency events.
  6. The rules' errors come from the measurement chain. The rules made {{results.derived.test_shift__rules__errors|int}} errors on test_shift ({{results.derived.test_iid__rules__errors|int}} on test_iid), {{results.derived.test_shift__rules__jump_to_dip_swell|int}} of them phase jumps whose half-cycle RMS crossed 0.90/1.10 transiently; the others come from calibration error and the unseen transient. Fixed IEC thresholds are robust to noise but sensitive to calibration — which is why the C2000 integration makes calibration mandatory.
  7. Embedded cost does not decide. The tree model adds about {{results.embedded_cost.gbm_comparisons_per_record_mean.test_iid|int}} comparisons and {{results.embedded_cost.gbm_model_bytes|int}} bytes of flash to a {{results.embedded_cost.dsp_features_ops_per_record|int}}-operation feature pipeline that both methods share. Robustness decides.

Limitations

  • Synthetic data from this project's own generator: training, validation and both test sets share its physics; the shift is one designed nuisance configuration. The rules were written by people who know the generator.
  • Strong-grid assumption; no converter or PCS dynamics; single-label records with label margins (no multi-event or threshold-straddling records).
  • No measured data (no [EXP] evidence); timings are from a PC.
  • Validation was saturated (many GBM configurations tied at the top), so model selection could not prefer robust models; the GBM was trained without noise augmentation.

What would change the conclusion

  • A GBM trained on shift-like conditions (wider SNR and calibration ranges) that then matches the rules on new shift-like data: the finding would become "ML needs representative training conditions". Tested in Experiment 2.
  • Restricting the model to physically motivated features or monotonic constraints (physics subset: Experiment 2).
  • Measured PCS records with real noise, converter dynamics, calibration drift and multi-event cases — the rules' fixed thresholds could fail there too.
  • A confidence-gated hybrid (use the model only when it is confident, otherwise the rules), evaluated on new sealed data (the current test sets are no longer blind). Tested in Experiment 2.

Experiment 2 (Stage 2): does ML catch up with representative training data?

Stage 1 left one obvious objection: the GBM never saw low SNR or calibration error during training. Experiment 2 (S2E1) was pre-registered (commit {{s2e1.commits.prereg}}) before any of its data existed: the same GBM recipe was retrained on the full nuisance ranges (SNR 25-60 dB, ±2 % gain and offset, wider frequency, more harmonics and unbalance; no transients), once with all 37 features (GW) and once with a physics-only subset (GP, {{s2e1.selected.GP.n_features|int}} features), frozen (commit {{s2e1.commits.freeze}}) and evaluated once on two new sealed sets of {{s2e1.sets.test2_shift.n|int}} records: test2_shift (the Stage 1 shift conditions with new random draws, incl. the unseen transient) and test2_novel (training-like conditions plus a nuisance no method has seen: an interharmonic of 0.5-2 % between 70 and 950 Hz in half of the records). The Stage 1 rules (R) and GBM (G0) were not changed. Hybrids use the GBM only when its top probability is at least 0.5, otherwise the rules. Every number here comes from reports/s2e1_results.json; all are [SIM].

Selected models: GW {{s2e1.selected.GW.config.n_estimators|int}} stages × {{s2e1.selected.GW.config.max_leaf_nodes|int}} leaves, {{s2e1.selected.GW.bytes|int}} bytes; GP {{s2e1.selected.GP.config.n_estimators|int}} stages × {{s2e1.selected.GP.config.max_leaf_nodes|int}} leaves, {{s2e1.selected.GP.bytes|int}} bytes (both within the 64 KB budget). Experiment 2 findings, in plain words

  1. With representative training, the tree model no longer loses to the rules — it is slightly better. The pre-registered rule (confidence interval of the difference to the rules above zero on both new sets) gives "{{s2e1.decision.GW.verdict}}" for GW and "{{s2e1.decision.GP.verdict}}" for GP: GW − R {{s2e1.sets.test2_shift.comparisons.GW-R.diff|signed4}} (CI {{s2e1.sets.test2_shift.comparisons.GW-R.ci95|ci4}}) on test2_shift and {{s2e1.sets.test2_novel.comparisons.GW-R.diff|signed4}} (CI {{s2e1.sets.test2_novel.comparisons.GW-R.ci95|ci4}}) on test2_novel; GP − R {{s2e1.sets.test2_shift.comparisons.GP-R.diff|signed4}} and {{s2e1.sets.test2_novel.comparisons.GP-R.diff|signed4}}. The differences are small. Stage 1's "rules are more robust" becomes "ML needs representative training conditions".
  2. Stage 1 replicates on fresh data, and retraining removes the failure. On test2_shift the unchanged rules reach {{s2e1.sets.test2_shift.arms.R.macro_f1.mean|f4}} and the unchanged Stage 1 GBM {{s2e1.sets.test2_shift.arms.G0.macro_f1.mean|f4}}; the same recipe trained on the wide ranges gains {{s2e1.sets.test2_shift.comparisons.GW-G0.diff|signed3}}. The Stage 1 failure was a training-coverage problem.
  3. The unseen interharmonic is mostly harmless. Macro-F1 with minus without it: rules {{s2e1.survival.R.delta|signed4}}, GW {{s2e1.survival.GW.delta|signed4}}, GP {{s2e1.survival.GP.delta|signed4}} (all "{{s2e1.survival.GW.verdict}}" by the pre-registered rule). The intended look-alike of an oscillation appeared only for beats of 20-25 Hz ({{s2e1.sets.test2_novel.interharmonic_beat_secondary.R.20-25.n|int}} records): accuracy rules {{s2e1.sets.test2_novel.interharmonic_beat_secondary.R.20-25.value|f2}}, GW {{s2e1.sets.test2_novel.interharmonic_beat_secondary.GW.20-25.value|f2}}, GP {{s2e1.sets.test2_novel.interharmonic_beat_secondary.GP.20-25.value|f2}} — too few records for a claim.
  4. A confidence gate rescues a mis-trained model. The Stage 1 GBM used only when its top probability is at least 0.5 (otherwise the rules) rises from {{s2e1.sets.test2_shift.arms.G0.macro_f1.mean|f3}} to {{s2e1.sets.test2_shift.arms.H0.macro_f1.mean|f3}} on test2_shift, handing {{s2e1.sets.test2_shift.hybrid_ml_share.H0.0|pct0}} of the records to the model.
  5. Safety-critical confusions stay at zero (phase jump vs frequency event: GW {{s2e1.sets.test2_shift.arms.GW.critical_confusions_mean.phase_jump<->freq_event|f1}}, rules {{s2e1.sets.test2_shift.arms.R.critical_confusions_mean.phase_jump<->freq_event|f1}} on test2_shift; sag called normal: 0 for every arm).

Limits: one generator for training and test (test2_shift's ranges lie inside the wide training ranges; only its data and the transient are new); validation was saturated again, so the smallest model was selected; the rules were not re-tuned.

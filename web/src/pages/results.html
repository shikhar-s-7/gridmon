Results of the sealed test evaluation

Three methods were frozen on training/validation data (commit {{results.freeze_commit}}) and evaluated exactly once on two sealed test sets of {{results.splits.test_iid.n|int}} records each: test_iid (drawn like the training data) and test_shift (lower SNR, wider frequency range, more harmonics and unbalance, ±2 % calibration error and an unseen transient). Every number on this page comes from reports/results.json. All results are [SIM].

Headline

Macro-F1 averages the F1 score over the seven classes. ML methods: mean ± sd over three training seeds. False alarm = a normal record labelled as an event; miss = an event labelled normal.

Are the differences real?

Intervals: paired, class-stratified bootstrap 95 % confidence intervals of the macro-F1 difference (seed mean); McNemar: exact test on the records where exactly one of the two methods is right (per seed). "Better" is claimed only if the CI excludes zero.

Findings, in plain words

  1. On data like the training data, both are equally good. test_iid: GBM-embedded {{results.splits.test_iid.methods.gbm_embedded.macro_f1.mean|f4}} vs rules {{results.splits.test_iid.methods.rules.macro_f1.mean|f4}}; difference {{results.splits.test_iid.comparisons.gbm_embedded_minus_rules.diff|signed4}}, 95 % CI {{results.splits.test_iid.comparisons.gbm_embedded_minus_rules.ci95|ci4}} — no significant difference.
  2. Under shift the rules are significantly better. test_shift: rules {{results.splits.test_shift.methods.rules.macro_f1.mean|f4}}, GBM-embedded {{results.splits.test_shift.methods.gbm_embedded.macro_f1.mean|f4}} (difference {{results.splits.test_shift.comparisons.gbm_embedded_minus_rules.diff|signed4}}, CI {{results.splits.test_shift.comparisons.gbm_embedded_minus_rules.ci95|ci4}}). From iid to shift the rules lose {{results.iid_to_shift.rules.change|signed3}}, the tree model {{results.iid_to_shift.gbm_embedded.change|signed3}}. When noise and nuisances leave the training range, the learned model degrades sharply; the rules barely move.
  3. Where the model fails: phase jumps and false alarms at low SNR. GBM-embedded (seed 0) on test_shift recognised only {{results.derived.test_shift__gbm_embedded__jump_recall|pct0}} of phase jumps ({{results.derived.test_shift__gbm_embedded__jump_to_normal|int}} called normal, {{results.derived.test_shift__gbm_embedded__jump_to_osc|int}} oscillation) and flagged {{results.derived.test_shift__gbm_embedded__normal_flagged|int}} normal records ({{results.derived.test_shift__gbm_embedded__normal_to_osc|int}} as oscillation). Seed mean: {{results.splits.test_shift.methods.gbm_embedded.false_alarm_rate.mean|pct1}} false alarms, {{results.splits.test_shift.methods.gbm_embedded.miss_rate.mean|pct1}} misses. The model learned noise-dependent shortcuts; the physical jump threshold does not depend on noise.
  4. A bigger model did not help. GBM-unconstrained: test_iid {{results.splits.test_iid.methods.gbm_unconstrained.macro_f1.mean|f4}}, test_shift {{results.splits.test_shift.methods.gbm_unconstrained.macro_f1.mean|f4}} with {{results.splits.test_shift.methods.gbm_unconstrained.false_alarm_rate.mean|pct1}} false alarms.
  5. Safety-critical confusions stay at zero for the rules and GBM-embedded (phase jump vs frequency event, sag vs normal), while the naive PLL rule flags {{results.splits.test_shift.naive_pll.by_class.phase_jump.rate|pct0}} of phase jumps as frequency events.
  6. The rules' errors come from the measurement chain. The rules made {{results.derived.test_shift__rules__errors|int}} errors on test_shift ({{results.derived.test_iid__rules__errors|int}} on test_iid), {{results.derived.test_shift__rules__jump_to_dip_swell|int}} of them phase jumps whose half-cycle RMS crossed 0.90/1.10 transiently; the others come from calibration error and the unseen transient. Fixed IEC thresholds are robust to noise but sensitive to calibration — which is why the C2000 integration makes calibration mandatory.
  7. Embedded cost does not decide. The tree model adds about {{results.embedded_cost.gbm_comparisons_per_record_mean.test_iid|int}} comparisons and {{results.embedded_cost.gbm_model_bytes|int}} bytes of flash to a {{results.embedded_cost.dsp_features_ops_per_record|int}}-operation feature pipeline that both methods share. Robustness decides.

Limitations

  • Synthetic data from this project's own generator: training, validation and both test sets share its physics; the shift is one designed nuisance configuration. The rules were written by people who know the generator.
  • Strong-grid assumption; no converter or PCS dynamics; single-label records with label margins (no multi-event or threshold-straddling records).
  • No measured data (no [EXP] evidence); timings are from a PC.
  • Validation was saturated (many GBM configurations tied at the top), so model selection could not prefer robust models; the GBM was trained without noise augmentation.

What would change the conclusion

  • A GBM trained on shift-like conditions (wider SNR and calibration ranges) that then matches the rules on new shift-like data: the finding would become "ML needs representative training conditions".
  • Restricting the model to physically motivated features or monotonic constraints.
  • Measured PCS records with real noise, converter dynamics, calibration drift and multi-event cases — the rules' fixed thresholds could fail there too.
  • A confidence-gated hybrid (use the model only when it is confident, otherwise the rules), evaluated on new sealed data (the current test sets are no longer blind).
